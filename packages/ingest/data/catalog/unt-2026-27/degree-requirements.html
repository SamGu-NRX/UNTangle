Other Programs Offered

Grad Track

Certain majors and minors at UNT offer a Grad Track option for exceptional students. See the individual program for specific requirements. Students:

  • Submit application through the major department after completing at least 75 credit hours of coursework with a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.5
  • May take up to 12 hours of graduate credit toward the undergraduate degree
  • May begin taking graduate-level courses after completing 90 credit hours of coursework
  • Must complete the bachelor’s degree within one academic year of taking their first graduate course
  • Must apply for admission to the graduate program, generally by the end of the first term in the Grad Track program

See the Majors, Minors, Certificates  page for a complete list of Grad Track programs.

Teacher Certification

Students may take the coursework required for teacher certification through the University of North Texas, though UNT does not award the certificate. The Texas Education Agency awards teaching certificates under the authority of the State Board of Education, contingent upon the recommendation of the UNT College of Education. For more information, see the Educator Preparation Office in the College of Education.

Second Bachelor’s Degree

Students may complete a second bachelor’s degree after they have earned a bachelor’s degree from an institution whose accreditation is recognized by the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board or an equivalent credential from a foreign institution recognized by UNT. Students earning a second bachelor’s degree must meet all current graduation and program-specific requirements, including a minimum of 24 new hours. These 24 hours must:

  • Include 12 advanced hours in a field different from the major of the first bachelor’s degree
  • Be completed once the second bachelor’s degree has been declared

Students are advised by the college that offers the second bachelor’s degree and should contact Graduate Admissions for a second bachelor’s application.

Undergraduate Academic Certificates

The University of North Texas offers upper-division undergraduate academic certificates to meet workforce needs, provide students with life/career skills and knowledge, and allow for specialization in academic disciplines. For a list of undergraduate academic certificates, see the Majors, Minors, Certificates  page. Students may pursue an undergraduate academic certificate if they meet regular UNT admission requirements and meet the minimum academic standards for the academic discipline.

  • Undergraduate academic certificates require 12–20 semester credit hours, the majority of which must be advanced
  • Students are responsible for all prerequisites specified in course requirements, even if the prerequisite is not part of the certificate
  • All hours required for the certificate are to be completed at UNT

Note: Hours used for attaining an undergraduate academic certificate could exclude a student from consideration for the $1,000 Tuition Rebate since all hours earned for the undergraduate academic certificate are counted in the total hours earned toward a degree.

Degree Terminology

Concentration

A concentration is a minimum of 18 semester credit hours of coursework in a recognized area of study within the academic discipline. Concentrations are included on UNT transcripts and diplomas.

Double Degree

A double degree requires the completion of the specified requirements for two different degree types (e.g., Bachelor of Science, Bachelor of Arts) at UNT. Students must meet all current graduation and program-specific requirements, including the major requirement (in both degrees) of a minimum of 24 unique hours, including 12 hours of advanced work. Major courses beyond the minimum requirement of 24 unique hours, may apply toward both degrees, if applicable. Students must graduate with both degrees simultaneously. If one degree is completed before the other, the second degree requires admission to post-baccalaureate status.

Double Major

A double major requires the completion of a minimum of 24 semester credit hours unique to each major, including 12 hours of advanced work. Both majors must fall under a single degree type (e.g., Bachelor of Science). A second major is not considered a concentration.

Dual Degree

A dual degree allows a student to study at two or more institutions simultaneously and earn a degree from both institutions. Dual degrees are established programs as shown in the catalog.

Emphasis

An emphasis is less than 12 semester credit hours of coursework in a focused area of study within an academic discipline. Emphases are not included on UNT transcripts or diplomas.

Grad Track

A Grad Track allows undergraduate students working toward their first bachelor’s degree to apply up to 12 credit hours of approved graduate level courses to the requirements of an undergraduate degree and then transfer those credits to the graduate degree.

Major

A major requires a minimum of 24 semester hours in a given subject, including 12 hours of advanced work.

Minor

A minor requires at least 18 semester hours in a given subject, including 6 hours of advanced work. Specific course requirements are determined by the department offering the minor. Not all degrees require a minor.

Specialization

A specialization is a minimum of 12 semester credit hours of coursework in a focused area of study within an academic discipline. Specializations are not included on UNT transcripts or diplomas.

Degree Requirements

Students working toward a bachelor’s degree at the University of North Texas must meet certain minimum requirements to be awarded the degree. Schools, colleges and departments may have additional requirements to qualify for graduation. The university minimums are below. Students should refer to the individual programs for a complete listing of the requirements and total number of hours prescribed for the specific degree.

Students complete the degree requirements listed in the catalog published the year they are admitted to the university (i.e., students admitted for Fall 2026 meet the requirements for the 2026-27 catalog), unless they have spoken with an advisor to change their catalog year. Students may find their catalog year on the degree audit. See “Graduation Under a Particular Catalog” in the Graduation   section of this catalog for information about catalog options.

Minimum Hour Requirements

Students must complete a minimum of 120 semester hours to earn a degree at UNT. One course may be used to meet multiple requirements. Students should speak to an advisor to determine which courses can be used to meet more than one requirement. Schools, colleges and departments may have additional requirements to qualify for graduation. Students must complete:

  • University Core Curriculum: 42 hours (See University Core Curriculum  for the full requirements.)
  • Major: minimum of 24 semester hours, at least 12 advanced
  • Advanced Hours: minimum of 36 semester hours
  • Residence: minimum of 25 percent of the total hours for the degree (e.g., 30 hours for a 120-hour degree) at UNT

Advanced-Hour Credit

Advanced-hour credit is earned for courses taken at the 3000- or 4000-level:

  • A lower-level course that is substituted for a UNT upper-level course may not be used to satisfy advanced-hour requirements
  • A lower-level course that, when transferred, is determined to be equivalent to a UNT upper-level course does not satisfy the requirement of advanced hours

Residence

Courses taken in residence are courses taken at UNT. Online courses offered through UNT may be used toward the residence requirement. This is separate from the residence used to determine in-state vs. out-of-state tuition. See Financial Information  for more information about residence in regard to tuition and fees. Students must complete:

  • A minimum of 24 hours of advanced work (3000 or 4000 level) at UNT
  • A minimum of 12 hours of advanced work in the major (except for the BAAS degree) at UNT

Grade Requirements

Students must meet certain grade requirements, both overall and in certain courses, to show proficiency in their course work and be awarded a degree at UNT.

English Composition Proficiency

Students must have a grade of C or better in courses that meet the Communication requirement of the University Core Curriculum  to show proficiency in English composition. Students who have earned a D in one or more of these two required courses must repeat the course and raise the grade to a C or better. Transfer students with more than 90 hours and who have earned a D in any of the basic English writing courses must retake the course during their first term/semester in residence.

Grade Point Average (GPA)

Students must meet both overall and cumulative GPA requirements to earn a degree at UNT. Schools, colleges and departments may have additional GPA requirements for specific degrees.

  • A minimum overall grade point average (OGPA) of 2.000 (C) on all work attempted, including all transfer, correspondence, extension and residence work.
  • A minimum cumulative GPA (UNT-CGPA) of 2.000 (C) on all work at UNT. Students may not use transfer credit to raise the GPA of work done at UNT.

College or School Requirements

Students must satisfactorily complete all degree requirements specified by the school or college in which the degree is offered. The college/school/department/academic program requirements may exceed the university minimum requirements.

Individual academic programs may require specific courses that also satisfy part of the University Core Curriculum. Students may be required to take more than the minimum number of hours required for the degree if they choose to meet the University Core Curriculum requirement using a different course.

Elective

An elective is a course not specifically required for a degree that is taken to reach the minimum number of hours needed for degree completion. Some majors include program-specific electives, where students choose from a list of courses. Electives may also be used to meet the advanced hour requirement of the degree. Students should speak to an advisor to determine the number of elective hours available in their degree and to determine which courses will best suit their degree and interests.

Degree Tools

Degree Audit

A degree audit is an official document prepared in the office of the student’s academic dean that lists courses completed, courses to be completed, proficiency examinations and all other requirements for a particular degree. Students should have a degree audit prepared no later than the beginning of the junior year.

Four-Year Plans

A four-year plan is an example of one way a student may schedule courses to complete their degree in four years. Students can find examples of four-year plans on the Registrar’s Office Undergraduate Four-year Plans and Transfer Guides (Course Sequencing Maps) page. The plans include:

  • A suggested sequence for completing the required coursework in four years
  • Texas Common Course Numbering System equivalents to assess how lower-level courses transferred from a Texas college or university might apply to the degree
  • Additional information relevant for graduation
